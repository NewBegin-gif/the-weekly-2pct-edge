How the bot works: its dashboard and the decisions behind it

A bitcoin bot that trades BTC-EUR on Bitvavo with real money. Below: its dashboard as it was a day ago, how one trade runs, why it is bitcoin, and every design decision with the reason behind it.

01 Dashboard

The dashboard, at least 24 hours old

This is what the bot itself sees, in percentages and BTC prices. It is a snapshot from at least 24 hours ago, so it is never a signal to trade on.

The dashboard snapshot is not available in this build. It appears once the bot's read-only dashboard has data that is at least 24 hours old.

02 Mechanics

How one trade runs

  1. The ladder waits

    Limit buy orders sit below the price at fixed distances: a fine and a coarse ladder.

  2. A dip fills a rung

    When the price falls, an order fills. It becomes a position with its own buy price.

  3. Each position gets its own sale

    Straight after, a limit sell order waits a few percent above that buy price.

  4. Only sold at a profit

    A position is never sold below its buy price. What is down, waits.

  5. The weekly figure

    Only what is sold counts in the weekly result, after fees on both sides.

05 Design

The design decisions, and why

Every rule in the bot has a history. These are the main ones, with their date, what was measured, and how one sentence to your AI changes it.

  1. From the logbook

    A 0.5% weekly goal, not 2%

    The name promised 2% a week. Measured honestly, week after week, 0.5% turned out to be the realistic goal. The chart on the logbook draws both lines.

    Tell your AI “Report every week against a goal of 0.3%.”

  2. 5 Aug 2026

    Book the real fee on every fill

    Fees took roughly a fifth of gross profit between late April and early August. The bot now books the actual fee per fill, and since 10 Oct 2026 it also fills in fees the exchange settles later.

    Tell your AI “Use my own fee tier when you calculate sell targets.”

  3. Measured 5 Aug 2026

    Switch off time-decay

    A rule lowered the sell target as a position aged. In 78% of the 152 sales it touched, the original target was still reached (median: 3 hours later). It gave up about 0.33% per trade, so it was switched off.

    Tell your AI “Lower the sell target by 0.1% for each day a position stays open.”

  4. 9 Aug 2026

    An RSI taper instead of a hard block

    Blocking all buys above RSI 70 also froze the ladder, so a later shallow dip caught nothing. It became a taper: smaller orders between RSI 70 and 80, a hard block only above 80. The dashboard shows whether the brake is on right now.

    Tell your AI “Pause the ladder completely whenever RSI is above 75.”

  5. 20 Aug 2026; stop-loss and timers removed 26–29 Sep 2026

    Never sell at a loss

    A position is only sold above its buy price; stop-losses and position timers are gone. The price of that choice: losses don't disappear, they wait in open positions. In a deep drop the open number grows before anything shows up in the weekly figure.

    Tell your AI “Add a stop-loss at −15% for the fine ladder only.”

  6. 2 Sep 2026

    Real limit orders on the exchange

    Sell targets became real limit orders at the exchange instead of prices the bot watched. The buy ladder also places real limit orders: up to 20 fine and 10 coarse rungs below the price. A resting order is filled at its own price.

    Tell your AI “Use 10 fine rungs and keep them 0.2% apart.”

  7. 2 Oct 2026

    A depth budget for buying

    How much of the capital the bot may have in bitcoin depends on how deep the price is below the highest open purchase: 50% at that top, rising in a straight line to 100% at 25% below it. Capital is kept back for deeper dips.

    Tell your AI “Never put more than 60% of my capital in, at any price.”

  8. September 2026

    Fewer moving parts

    Several layers were removed: a machine-learning predictor, an anomaly detector, a strategy tournament and more. Most of them turned out not to change the orders the bot placed. Simpler code is easier to check, for you and for your AI.

    Tell your AI “Explain every rule in the code to me in one sentence each.”
