Toggl: price, catch and who it suits

Time tracking and capacity planning for freelancers, consultants and agencies, with budgets, rates and profitability per project. Relaunched from Toggl Track to Toggl in 2026.

The Weekly 2% Edge looks for small, repeatable gains in how a business runs. This page summarises our research on Toggl, read from the vendor’s own pages and from what other buyers report. It is not a hands-on test.

What it costs

Free plan; Starter $9 per user a month billed annually, or $12 monthly. Read on Toggl’s own pricing page on 2026-09-09; check the live page before you buy.

Toggl's honesty problem is the good kind: the free tier is so capable that many teams never need to pay — the paid line is drawn exactly at 'do you bill clients' (budgets, rates, profitability). If you do, Starter earns itself fast; if you track time for personal discipline, resist the upgrade. Time tracking's real cost everywhere is adoption: the tool is frictionless, but the habit still has to be built.

Who it suits

Freelancers, consultants and agencies that bill by the hour and want frictionless tracking with budgets, rates and profitability per project.

Who should skip it

Skip it if you need surveillance-style monitoring (screenshots, activity scoring — deliberately not Toggl's philosophy), or free-tier tracking already covers you.

Getting started

Answer one question before you look at plans: do you bill anyone by the hour? If yes, Starter is the plan — billable rates and revenue reporting are exactly what sits behind the paywall, and one recovered unbilled hour covers the year. If no, stay free; the free tier is a real product rather than a trial and a great many teams never need more.
